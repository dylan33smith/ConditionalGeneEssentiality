### Assumption Under Test
- assumption_statement: H-R-CHEM-01 — under within-org condition-holdout ranking,
  structural fingerprints (Morgan / RDKit / MACCS) outperform the 425-dim
  multihot chemistry baseline (reversing the T6-A result, which was under MSE +
  cross-org). Secondary: any chemistry arm with the locked T5-A architecture
  beats the gene-specific chemistry-kNN baseline.
- assumption_type: representation
- why_it_matters: R1 is the first R-regime model tier; it tests whether the
  chemistry encoder is a lever for the ranking task, and whether the locked
  deep model beats the simple non-parametric baseline at all.

### Pre-Registered Test Plan
- comparison: 6 chemistry arms (multihot_425, morgan_2048, rdkit_2048, maccs_167,
  morgan_plus_multihot, maccs_plus_multihot) × 3 seeds, 23 replicate orgs.
- fixed_controls: R-LOCK-1 eligibility (tail_g/w_g), R-LOCK-2 condition-holdout
  split, R-LOCK-3 pointwise sampler, R-LOCK-4 metric; T5-A locked architecture;
  weighted pointwise MSE; 8 epochs; Spearman early-stop.
- metrics_primary: [within_gene_spearman, ndcg_at_5]
- promotion_rule: Δ-Spearman ≥ 0.023 vs chem-kNN AND beat chem-kNN on NDCG@5,
  with BH-FDR across the 6 arms; must also beat the chem-NULL gate.

### Evidence Summary

**Per-arm model metrics (mean ± std across 3 seeds, 23 orgs, common eligible
val gene set ~46k genes):**

| arm | Spearman | NDCG@1 | NDCG@5 | prec@5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| multihot_425 | 0.130 ± 0.002 | 0.310 | **0.422** ± 0.001 | 0.392 |
| morgan_2048 | 0.125 ± 0.004 | 0.294 | 0.415 ± 0.004 | 0.387 |
| rdkit_2048 | 0.110 ± 0.010 | 0.283 | 0.406 ± 0.003 | 0.384 |
| maccs_167 | 0.118 ± 0.003 | 0.295 | 0.414 ± 0.003 | 0.387 |
| morgan_plus_multihot | 0.126 ± 0.005 | 0.306 | 0.420 ± 0.001 | 0.388 |
| maccs_plus_multihot | 0.129 ± 0.002 | 0.305 | 0.419 ± 0.005 | 0.391 |
| **chem-kNN (BAR)** | **0.240** | **0.375** | **0.485** | **0.434** |
| chem-NULL (floor) | 0.023 | 0.240 | 0.343 | 0.345 |
| noise-floor (ceiling) | 0.393 | 0.558 | 0.658 | 0.604 |

**Findings:**
1. **Fingerprints do NOT beat multihot.** multihot_425 is the BEST arm
   (NDCG@5 0.422); every fingerprint arm is slightly worse (morgan 0.415,
   rdkit 0.406, maccs 0.414; hybrids ~0.42). H-R-CHEM-01 is **rejected** —
   same direction as T6-A under MSE+cross-org. Structural priors did not help.
2. **NO arm beats the chem-kNN bar.** The best model (multihot, NDCG@5 0.422,
   Spearman 0.130) loses to chem-kNN (0.485 / 0.240) by a wide, consistent
   margin (3-seed std ≤ 0.01). The gap is far larger than the 0.023 promotion
   delta in the WRONG direction.
3. **FDR is moot.** No arm is even a promotion candidate (all lose to kNN
   pre-correction); BH-FDR across arms changes nothing.
4. The deep model beats the chem-NULL floor (0.422 vs 0.343 NDCG@5) — it has
   *some* signal — but the simple gene-specific kNN is decisively better.

### Decision
- decision_outcome: **no_winner (PROVISIONAL).** Carry **multihot_425** forward
  as the held-constant chemistry encoder for R2 and R-LOSS (best arm here AND
  the T-regime lock). Fingerprint arms not promoted. **The fingerprint question
  is DEFERRED, not killed** — re-tested in an **R1-revisit** under the R2+R-LOSS
  winning architecture+loss (see next_action).
- rationale: The chemistry *encoder* is not the lever **under the current
  (MSE + T5-A) setup** — all six encodings cluster at NDCG@5 ≈ 0.41–0.42, below
  the chem-kNN bar (0.485). But R1 was CONFOUNDED: it varied the encoder while
  holding fixed two things we suspect are suboptimal (pointwise MSE loss, T5-A
  fusion). The honest reading is "encoder doesn't matter *under MSE + T5-A*",
  NOT "encoder never matters." Because chemistry encoder may interact with
  fusion (R2) and loss (R-LOSS), the encoder verdict is provisional until those
  are improved.
  - Counter-evidence (why a revisit may re-confirm multihot): fingerprints were
    uniformly *worse*, not merely no-better; and S1's ≥95% chemistry overlap
    means held-out conditions are recombinations of *seen* chemicals, so
    multihot's exact-match bits are already lossless and fingerprints' structural
    smoothness has little novel chemistry to generalize to. A revisit is cheap
    and makes the final claim airtight, but multihot is the likely outcome.
  - The decisive finding is architectural/objective, not representational: the
    locked T5-A model under pointwise MSE cannot beat a non-parametric
    gene-specific chemistry-similarity lookup. The bottleneck is fusion and
    the loss, not the chemistry features.
- risks_remaining:
  - Single split seed (R-LOCK-2 seed 0); 3 seeds vary only model init. A
    split-seed sweep would tighten the estimate but is unlikely to change the
    qualitative verdict given the large, consistent gap.
  - chem-kNN's strength may partly reflect the transductive-over-genes structure
    (it uses each gene's own train fits). A model that genuinely beats it would
    be strong evidence of learned generalization beyond lookup.
- next_action:
  1. Proceed to **R2 (fusion topology)** and **R-LOSS (ranking loss)** — the two
     levers this result implicates — holding the encoder at multihot_425.
  2. **R1-revisit (deferred):** after R2 + R-LOSS lock a better architecture+loss,
     re-run the 6 chemistry arms under that winner. Only then finalize the
     R-regime chemistry encoder. (Cheap: one 6-arm sweep on the existing pipeline.)
  3. **chem-kNN becomes the promotion-gate baseline** for R2, R-LOSS, and all
     subsequent R-tiers: NDCG@5 0.485 / Spearman 0.240 is the bar a model must
     clear to claim it beats a non-parametric lookup. (Currently the strong
     baseline; this result argues it should gate — pending sign-off.)
  Ordering note: R-LOSS arguably precedes R2 — optimizing pointwise MSE for a
  ranking metric is the more fundamental mismatch. Either order is acceptable
  since the R1-revisit happens after BOTH.

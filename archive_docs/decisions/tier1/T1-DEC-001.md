### Assumption Under Test
- assumption_statement: Decomposed chemistry encoding — a multihot over the
  locked 425-slot Canonical_ID vocab covering both medium and stressor
  chemistry per experiment — beats coarse medium-name-only encoding on the
  locked split (`multi_org_balanced`) by the S2-locked thresholds (0.0212 RMSE,
  0.005 MAE) on both co-primaries.
- assumption_type: representation
- why_it_matters: H-ENC-01 is the foundational T1 hypothesis. If chemistry
  decomposition doesn't help, T1-B through T1-E and all downstream tiers are
  building on a representation that adds engineering complexity without
  measured gain.

#### Structural caveat that drives the no_winner outcome

`multi_org_balanced` has `val_seen_rate = 1.0` at the media-name level — every
val medium also appears in train. So the media_id encoder never falls back to
UNK; it has a learnable 425-dim embedding for every val medium. The S1-predicted
advantage of multihot (generalizing across organism-unique medium names) is
**not exercised by this protocol**. H-ENC-01 is structurally under-stress-tested
here.

### Decision

- decision_outcome: **no_winner** for the inter-arm comparison.
- promote_to_T2: **multihot_canonical_id** as the locked T1 representation,
  by tiebreaker rules:
  1. multihot wins on RMSE on every individual organism (4/4) consistently.
  2. multihot beats best_non_global baseline by ~0.07 RMSE (H-BASE-01
     passes with ample margin).
  3. multihot generalizes to protocols with unseen val media (a property
     `multi_org_balanced` doesn't test but `largest_by_rows` does);
     media_id structurally cannot.
  4. multihot is what S5's locked policy already uses; not changing the
     encoder keeps the substrate aligned with locked S5 numbers.
- The H-ENC-01 hypothesis as written is **not falsified** by this
  experiment; it is **not adequately tested** by `multi_org_balanced`.
  See "Risks remaining" below.

- rationale: tiebreakers consistently favor multihot. The MAE advantage of
  media_id is real but small and only on the central-mass metric;
  multihot's tail-resistance is the property that matters more for the
  project's downstream needs (heavy-tailed Tn-seq fitness, H-LOSS-01
  motivation for Huber loss in T4).

- risks_remaining:
  1. The H-ENC-01 hypothesis as written is **not adequately tested** by
     `multi_org_balanced`. The protocol has 0% UNK rate on media-name
     lookups, so the media_id encoder benefits from full per-medium
     coverage. A protocol with unseen val media (e.g. `largest_by_rows`
     with val_seen_rate = 0%) would actually stress-test the
     decomposed-vs-name distinction. **Follow-up filed in REFACTORPLAN §12.**
  2. Media_id's MAE advantage (0.006) is small but consistent. Possible
     mechanism: media_id has 113 × 425 ≈ 48k trainable params for the
     condition encoder; multihot's projection is implicit through the
     concat-linear MLP. If T1-B / T2 widen the chemistry projection,
     this gap may close or flip.
  3. The homology-bin crossover suggests that a hybrid encoder
     (chemistry-weighted by homology) might beat both arms. Goes to T2
     fusion-tier consideration.

- next_action:
  - Lock `multihot_canonical_id` as the T1 condition encoder for T1-B,
    T1-C, T1-D, T1-E.
  - Emit `data_contract/representation_winner.yaml`.
  - Open REFACTORPLAN §12 entry for "T1-A.2: same comparison on
    largest_by_rows" (stress-test H-ENC-01 properly).
  - Carry the homology-bin crossover to T2 as a candidate fusion
    motivation.

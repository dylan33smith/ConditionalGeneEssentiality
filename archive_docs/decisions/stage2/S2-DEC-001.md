### Assumption Under Test
- assumption_statement: For each candidate protocol, the locked null-baseline
  suite + Spearman eligibility policy + meaningful-gain thresholds together
  give us a trustworthy evaluation framework — meaning a real model's
  improvement against these baselines is detectable, not noise.
- assumption_type: evaluation
- why_it_matters: every later tier promotion gate consumes this. If the gate
  is too lax, we promote noise. If too strict, we never promote.

### Pre-Registered Test Plan
- comparison: 5 baselines per protocol × 4 protocols, plus power analysis
  (eligibility, bootstrap CI, permutation null), plus heteroscedastic-noise
  diagnostics on additive residuals.
- success criteria:
  - all 5 baselines computable per protocol with documented fallback rates,
  - bootstrap CI half-width < 0.05 for at least one protocol (otherwise
    Spearman is diagnostic-only across the board),
  - permutation null clearly separated from "real signal" mass,
  - per-protocol gain thresholds exist and are non-degenerate.

### Evidence Summary

#### Baseline metrics (RMSE)

| Protocol | global | per_cond | per_org | additive | NN | NN fallback |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| largest_by_rows | 0.5668 | 0.5742 | 0.5668 | 0.5675 | 0.6060 | 100% |
| high_overlap_easy | 0.5866 | 0.5875 | 0.5866 | 0.5873 | 0.6251 | 32.7% |
| low_overlap_stress | 0.9370 | 0.9386 | 0.9370 | 0.9388 | 0.9793 | 100% |
| multi_org_balanced | 0.6307 | 0.6319 | 0.6307 | 0.6320 | **0.5884** | 0% |

**Critical finding: 4 of 5 baselines collapse to global_train_mean under
organism-holdout.** Per-condition, per-organism, and additive baselines all
fall back to the global mean for held-out organism rows because every val row
is a cold-start (the organism, the gene, and almost always the expName are
all unseen in train). Only `embedding_nn` is meaningfully different — and
only when val media are also in train.

#### Power analysis (additive baseline within-gene Spearman)

| Protocol | n_eligible (m=5) | mean Spearman | bootstrap 95% CI | half-width | role |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|:---:|
| largest_by_rows | 3041 | 0.0098 | [0.0068, 0.0128] | 0.0030 | primary |
| high_overlap_easy | 6095 | −0.0014 | [−0.0031, 0.0003] | 0.0017 | primary |
| low_overlap_stress | 1424 | −0.0119 | [−0.0192, −0.0046] | 0.0073 | primary |
| multi_org_balanced | 13804 | 0.0056 | [0.0019, 0.0092] | 0.0036 | primary |

All half-widths < 0.01, all n_eligible ≥ 200. **Power is not the bottleneck:**
we can detect a real model improvement of ≥0.02 Spearman on any protocol.

#### Permutation null (200 permutations per protocol)

null p95 < 0.01 across all 4 protocols → any real-model Spearman > 0.02 is
clearly above-chance.

#### Heteroscedastic-noise diagnostics

Additive-baseline residual quantiles (per protocol):
- P5: −1.4 to −2.4 (long left tail)
- P95: 0.7 to 1.0 (modest right tail)
- Strongly asymmetric — confirms heavy-tailed `fit` distribution from S1 fig 13.
- Per-organism residual std varies 2–5× within a single protocol.

This validates **L4 (RMSE+MAE co-primary)** and motivates **H-LOSS-01 (Huber > MSE)**.

### Decision

#### Outcome
**Approved with two policy revisions to handle the cold-start collapse:**

#### Revision 1: gain-threshold rule (replaces `eval_policy.yaml`'s original rule)

Old rule (auto-computed during S2 run): `0.5 × (global_mean − additive)`.
This produced **negative thresholds** because additive ≈ global mean under
organism-holdout. The rule was based on a false premise (that additive
captures non-trivial signal).

**New rule:**

    threshold = max(0.005, 0.5 × |global_mean − best_non_global_baseline|)

where `best_non_global_baseline` is the minimum-RMSE (resp. MAE) baseline
among `{per_condition_mean, per_organism_mean, additive_baseline, embedding_nn}`.

Resulting per-protocol thresholds (RMSE):

| Protocol | best_non_global | gap | threshold |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| largest_by_rows | global itself (0.567) | 0 | 0.005 (floor) |
| high_overlap_easy | global itself (0.587) | 0 | 0.005 (floor) |
| low_overlap_stress | global itself (0.937) | 0 | 0.005 (floor) |
| multi_org_balanced | NN (0.588) | 0.043 | **0.0212** |

For 3 of 4 protocols, no non-global baseline beats global mean — the threshold
defaults to the 0.005 floor. For `multi_org_balanced`, NN beats global by
0.043, so the threshold is half that gap.

The 0.005 floor is conservative: 2× the bootstrap CI half-width for the
tightest CI we observed (0.0017). A real improvement of 0.005 RMSE is
detectable at the bootstrap noise level.

#### Revision 2: H-BASE-01 reinterpretation

Original framing: "model must beat additive baseline → proves it learns
gene×condition interactions."

Under organism-holdout, additive ≈ global mean (because val genes/conditions
are cold-start). "Beats additive" reduces to "beats a constant." This is
a much weaker bar than the original spirit.

**Revised gate:** a model must beat the **best non-global baseline** by
the gain threshold (above) on RMSE AND MAE.

- For `multi_org_balanced`: must beat embedding_nn (0.588 RMSE) by ≥0.021.
- For all other protocols: must beat global_mean by ≥0.005 (since no
  non-global baseline beats global there).

H-BASE-01 retained as a sanity check ("did the model at least match additive?")
but is no longer the binding gate under organism-holdout.

#### Risks remaining
- The "0.005 floor" is a designer's choice. If it turns out tier models routinely
  improve by 0.003–0.004 RMSE consistently across seeds, we've set the bar too
  high and would reject real improvements. The floor can be revised in a future
  ledger entry if seed-stability tests in T1 show the threshold is too coarse.
- `embedding_nn` only beats global mean on 1 of 4 protocols. On the other 3,
  the gate effectively reduces to "beat global mean by 0.005." This is a weak
  bar. Tier results should be reported with explicit "beats which baseline by
  what margin" rather than just "passed gate."
- Per-organism residual std varies up to 5× within a single protocol →
  averaged metrics will hide per-organism failure modes. Tier reports must
  include per-organism panels.

#### Next action
- Update `data_contract/policy/eval_policy.yaml` to use the revised gain-threshold
  rule (already patched).
- S3 selects the locked primary protocol from these 4 candidates. The decision
  ledger entry for S3 must reference this evidence.
